Designing a Humane and Ethical Internet: Putting People Before Profit

February 17, 2025

To me, a fair and ethical internet is one where the user’s well-being comes first. It’s not something that should be added later, it should be the main goal. I started to understand this better after learning about the work of Tristan Harris and the Center for Humane Technology. They believe that websites and apps should be designed to protect people’s mental health, not just to make money. Right now, a lot of the internet feels like a trap. It pulls people in with endless scrolling, annoying pop-ups, and smart systems (called algorithms) that decide what we see without asking us.

That’s why I believe being open and honest, transparency, is so important. A more ethical internet would clearly explain how and why certain content is being shown to us. Algorithms shouldn’t work in the background without us knowing. As someone who uses the internet not only to learn but also to be creative, express myself, and connect with others, I feel that websites should be built on values like care, truthfulness, and respect. The Mozilla Foundation also talks about this in their work. They say we need to stop “dark patterns”, tricky designs that confuse users, and fix biased algorithms that treat some people unfairly, especially those from minority or marginalized groups. That really made me ask myself: if the internet is supposed to be for everyone, why does it feel like it only helps a few people?

Some of our course readings also made me think more deeply about this. For example, Walter Benjamin wrote about how mass production can take away the special feeling of original art. I think this idea connects to the internet today. When everything online is focused only on likes, views, and clicks, it can lose its true value. A better, more ethical internet would push against this. It would help people have real conversations and give them more control over their own information, instead of turning them into a product for advertisers.

I also think that coding and design are not just technical, they are political. Every choice we make as website designers, coders, or users can have big effects. These choices are never just neutral. That’s why I believe an ethical internet should be one where websites and apps are designed to be easy to understand, respectful to everyone, and give people more control. It’s about giving power back to the users, not only in how we use websites but also in how we see ourselves in the digital world. In the end, creating an ethical internet means building it around care, honesty, fairness, and real human needs. It’s about making sure technology supports people, not the other way around.
